01. Where It All Started

Apple's Story: From a Garage to a Tech Giant

1976 – 1984

The Garage & First Macs

Started April 1, 1976 by Jobs, Wozniak & Wayne. Apple I & II kicked off personal computers; Macintosh (1984) made the mouse & screen icons mainstream.

1985 – 1997

Near Bankruptcy & Comeback

After Steve Jobs was pushed out in 1985, messy product lines nearly sank Apple—until buying NeXT in 1997 brought Jobs back to save the company.

1998 – 2006

iMac, iPod & iTunes

The colorful iMac G3 (1998) put Apple back on the map, while iPod + iTunes (2001) changed how the world listened to music (400M+ sold).

2007

The iPhone Game-Changer

Steve Jobs unveiled the very first iPhone, turning Apple from a computer company into the king of mobile tech.

2010 – Present

Ecosystem & Custom Chips

Expansion into iPad, Apple Watch, AirPods, Apple's own ultra-fast M & A chips, and digital services ($3T+ value).

Jan 2019 • -$74B in 1 Day

2019 Stock Crash (-10%)

Tim Cook issued Apple's first sales warning since 2002, cutting Q1 expectations from ~$91B to $84B due to slower China sales and $29 battery swaps delaying upgrades—dropping ~38% from its peak before bouncing back.

2017 – 2020 • $500M Payout

"Batterygate" Slowdowns

Apple quietly slowed down older iPhones (iPhone 6, 6s, 7, SE) via iOS updates to stop random shutdowns without telling users, sparking huge backlash and a $500M US settlement.

2020 – Present • Legal Battles

App Store Fees & EU Rules

Lawsuits over the 30% App Store cut—including Epic Games v. Apple (the Fortnite ban), a US antitrust lawsuit, and EU rules forcing Apple to allow third-party app stores and USB-C.

2010 – 2012 • Early Drama

Foxconn & "Antennagate"

Criticism over tough working hours at Foxconn factories in China (leading to stricter factory checks) and the 2010 iPhone 4 signal drop issue ("You're holding it wrong", solved with free cases).

Plot Twists & Setbacks: Jan 2019 Stock Crash (-$74B in one day) • Batterygate ($500M payout) • Fortnite / App Store Lawsuits & EU Rules

How Apple Works

1. "Three Products in One"

  • Before 2007: Nokia and BlackBerry phones had tiny screens, dozens of plastic buttons, styluses, and slow, broken internet.
  • Multi-Touch Glass: Steve Jobs combined a widescreen iPod, a phone, and real desktop Safari browsing controlled purely by fingers.

2. Instant Launch-Day Hype

  • $400M+ Free Buzz: Global media excitement turned the iPhone into a cultural phenomenon months before it even hit shelves.
  • Overnight Lines: On June 29, 2007, thousands camped outside Apple Stores, buying 270,000 units in 30 hours.

3. App Store & $199 Price

  • 2008 App Store Boom: Opening the platform to developers created millions of apps and games—making iPhone essential every day.
  • Carrier Subsidies: Dropping the upfront price from $599 to $199 fueled mass adoption and >90% customer loyalty.

06. Business & Economics Playbook

Smart Business Moves Behind Apple's Profits

1. Premium Pricing (Price Skimming)

Instead of fighting cheap price wars, Apple sells clear tiers (Standard vs. Pro/Pro Max & storage upgrades), keeping a strong 45–47% profit margin.

Global Smartphone Market: Sales Share vs. Profit Share

2. Building Everything In-House

  • Custom Apple Chips: Designing its own A- & M-series chips means Apple doesn't pay middleman markups and gets better speed & battery life.
  • Bulk Buying Power: Ordering 230M+ iPhones a year lets Apple negotiate top deals and exclusive parts from suppliers.
  • Fast Inventory: Apple turns over stock in under 10 days, avoiding warehouses full of unsold phones.

07. Branding & Marketing

How Apple Markets Desire & Builds Hype

1. Selling Vibes, Not Just Specs

  • Emotion Over Numbers: Apple rarely brags about RAM or gigahertz—instead, ads focus on creativity, privacy, and lifestyle, so people gladly pay full price.
  • "Shot on iPhone" Billboards: Real photos taken by everyday users turn customers into free advertising around the world.

2. Secret Launches & Keynote Hype

  • September Launch Events: Apple's sleek video keynotes get billions of views and free news coverage globally without traditional ad spend.
  • Scarcity & FOMO: Strict pre-launch secrecy and launch-week sellouts trigger FOMO (Fear Of Missing Out) and make new iPhones feel must-have.

3. Iconic Stores & Resale Value

  • Apple Stores as Hangouts: Glass-walled flagship stores let anyone test devices hands-free and get fast Genius Bar help, earning top sales per square foot.
  • High Second-Hand Value: Used iPhones hold their price way better than rival phones, making yearly trade-in upgrades much cheaper.
"People don't buy what you do; they buy why you do it." — Apple's Think Different mindset #1 Most Valuable Global Brand (~$500B+ Brand Value)
